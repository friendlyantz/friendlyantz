## Purpose of this record

This page is a chronology of water ingress into **Unit 51, 13 The Esplanade, St Kilda VIC 3182**, apparently originating from **Unit 61** directly above. Every entry below is taken from the email chains between myself(Anton Panteleev, Lot/Unit 51), the Owners Corporation manager, and the managing agent for Unit 61. Dates and quotations are reproduced from those emails.

> **Note on redaction.** Third-party personal details (the Unit 61 tenant's name and contact details, the Unit 61 owner's name and email, direct mobile numbers) have been redacted from this public record. They are held in the unredacted email archive and can be produced to the Owners Corporation, an insurer, or VCAT on request.

### 2025: first report, first investigation, no resolution

**8 November 2025** — I report the leak for the first time. Email to Montana Burnett (MICM), copied to three other MICM staff:

> "I just discovered a leak in my bedroom ceiling — see attached. I think it is coming from u61, but not sure. It seems like this is a slow leak pooling under the ceiling and seaping through the brickwall nearby, which damaged my cabinetary, wall and ceiling… Can you please address this urgently as I am not sure it is safe to use the bedroom now given the mould damage. I might look to spend a few nights somewhere else."

**10 November 2025** — Montana confirms a work order has been raised for Distinction Plumbing to attend, noting I work from home and am flexible: *"we just need it attended ASAP."*

**17 November 2025** — Montana emails the owner of Unit 61 directly (marked **Importance: High**) seeking an access contact: *"We currently have plumbers investigating a leak into APT 51 through the roof space, and they require access to your unit to carry out a leak inspection."*

**18 November 2025** — George Matis provides the Unit 61 tenant's contact details to Montana and Distinction the following morning.

**18 November 2025** — Distinction reports it has attempted to call the Unit 61 tenant *"with not luck"* and has texted to seek access on Monday 24 November, 8–9am.

**21 November 2025** — I ask Montana for an update and raise damage for the first time: *"Any updates on the leak fix? Is it possible to get some compensation for couple of wall cabinets that i had to replace and making good the wall/ceilings?"*

**27 November 2025** — Plumbers attend the property above. Montana reports *"there does not appear to be a current active leak."* I confirm the area seems dry and install a leak sensor. Montana advises monitoring, and that the work order is **"standing"** so it can be re-attended if there is further movement.

> **Outcome of the 2025 round:** no cause identified, no repair, no remediation of the cabinetry, wall or ceiling damage already sustained. The matter is left on "monitor."

### 31 May – 17 June 2026: the source is identified, and nothing happens

**31 May 2026** — Major ingress resumes. I report **approximately 700ml within 24 hours** and continuing slow leakage, with photographs.

**1 June 2026** — Montana instructs Distinction to attend and arrange access to the unit above.

**2 June 2026** — I report water now seeping through the wall as well and **pooling on the floor and in the carpet**. approx 1000ml of water daily dripping through ceiling only. more water is seeping through the wall and pooling on the floor slab.

**3 June 2026** — I ask for the attendance to be expedited: *"flooding is pooling on the floor slab i believe, and there is approx 2sqm area of wet carpet now as well as damage to my property and furniture."* Distinction books a plumber for the following morning and confirms access to Unit 61 for 8–10am.

**4 June 2026** — **The root cause is identified.** OC plumbers attend and identify the source as **the hot water line in Unit 61**, in the toilet-basin area, and advise Unit 61 to address it urgently and to isolate or avoid using the hot water.

**9 June 2026** — Montana receives Distinction's report, invoice and images.

**10 June 2026** — I report the position five days after the cause was found:

> "While the ceiling leak has reduced, it is still ongoing. The wall continues to seep a substantial amount of water, which is flooding the floor slab, soaking the carpets, and damaging my furniture… My bedroom is becoming increasingly unlivable. If they can isolate the leak/hotwater for the time being, that would be very helpful."

**10 June 2026** — Montana sends the plumber's report, invoice and images to George with an explicit, twofold, urgent request:

> "Can you please arrange for a trade to attend and carry out further investigation **as a matter of urgency**? The Resident in the property below has noted that the leak is causing damage within their property including to their contents, so rectification is a priority.
>
> **In the interim, would you be able to arrange for isolation of the water line to the basin or request the tenant cease using it** whilst the investigation and repairs are completed?"

**12 June 2026** — Two days pass with no reply. I write to Montana:

> "Could you please ask the agent for Unit 61 to urgently address this and isolate the leaking water line as soon as possible? Unit 61 was advised to address this more than a week ago, and **it seems nothing has been done**. I have not been contacted about any progress, and the leak is still ongoing through the walls. I am unable to normally use my bedroom, the carpets are extremely wet, the room is getting mouldy and smelly, and this is causing ongoing damage to the structure, fitout and furniture."

**12 June 2026** — Montana follows up George a second time, now copying me in:

> "I am just touching base on my email below and if there are any updates on when this will be rectified or the hot water isolated? The Owner of the property below, which is currently suffering damages due to the leak, is included in this email."

> **State of play at the end of Snapshot 1:** the cause has been identified for 8 days. Unit 61 has been asked twice, in writing, to isolate the water line. There has been **no response from Unit 61's agent**, no isolation, no trade engaged, and no contact with me. The leak is ongoing and the bedroom is unlivable.

### 14 July 2026: Another Lot61/the Austest report

Austest Pipeline Solutions, **Leak Detection Report, Job No. 30345**, prepared for Plumbsmart at 61/13 The Esplanade, St Kilda:

- Records **water staining and damage to the ceiling and wall of Unit 51**, and confirms Unit 61 is directly above.
- Pressure tests to Unit 61's domestic hot and cold water lines **passed on the day**; no active escape was observed while onsite; thermal camera found no live evidence.
- Moisture testing found **high/elevated moisture**, concentrated around the right wall of the powder room and present on **both the powder-room and bedroom sides** of that wall.
- Confirms Unit 61's water pipes are **in close proximity** to the area where water entered Unit 51.
- Concludes the moisture and damage **"definitely indicate" a leak in recent times**, and that the moisture readings point to the **leak origin being on Level 6** — that is, Unit 61.
- Speculates a leaking mains pipe **may have temporarily sealed itself** with internal debris.
- Recommends either monitoring for renewed water entry and retesting, or exposing pipes in Unit 51's ceiling for visual monitoring.

> **Correction to the report's wording.** The report states water entry had not occurred *"for over a month."* That characterisation is disputed. The flooding began on **31 May 2026** and only substantially subsided after the first Plumbsmart attendance on **18 June 2026**. At the date of the report the area had been dry for approximately **four weeks** — a consequence of the leak's intermittent, self-sealing behaviour, not evidence that no leak occurred.

### Pattern

Across nine months, every step forward was initiated by me or by the Owners Corporation manager. On the record above, Unit 61's agent:

- did not respond to the OC manager's calls (March 2026) or emails (June 2026) in a reasonable time or without repeated follow-up;
- took six days to answer a one-line access question while a detectable leak was active (April 2026);
- did not isolate the water line after the cause was identified, despite two written requests (June 2026);
- made first contact with the affected owner only after seventeen days and a direct approach (June 2026);

The cumulative effect was that an intermittent leak, detectable in early June, was allowed to run until the affected room was un-livable — and remained un-repaired and un-remediated for months after the flooding began.

## Where responsibility sits

The Owners Corporation's plumbers identified the source as the hot water line in Unit 61 on **4 June 2026**.

Unit 61 was asked in writing to isolate that line early June 2026, and followed up again on **12 June 2026**. It was not isolated. The bulk of the damage recorded below accrued **after** those requests were made.

Agent only responded on the 17th June, when the substantial damage was done and water was pooling in the bedroom.

## Damage and impact

### Water ingress

- Repeated ingress through ceiling and wall areas from November 2025 onward, recurring in March, April, May and June 2026.
- Volumes recorded at the time of reporting: ~350–500ml/24h (March 2026), ~300ml/24h (April 2026), ~1L over a week's absence (May 2026), ~700-1000ml/24h (31 May 2026), with continuing seepage into mid-June 2026 pooling under the carpet, floowing bedroom floor completely
- Water pooling on the floor slab;
- structural wall completely saturated with water flooding toilet next doow, destroying tile wall, ceiling, vanity top

### Health

- Prolonged exposure to mould and damp conditions across **months** of continuous ingress and subsequent contamination — from the first mould observed in November 2025, through the sustained flooding of 31 May to 19 June 2026, and continuing while the affected materials remain in place.
- Bedroom rendered unlivable. Sleeping away from home from the week of 8 June 2026 (*"I have to sleep outside my home since last week"* — 17 June 2026).
- Health effects reported to Unit 61's agent on 11 August 2026 (*"the mould is substantially affecting my health now"*) and again on 13 August 2026 (*"It is substantially affecting my health now and i need to seek temporary accomodation until this is resolved"*).

### Building fabric and fitout

- Ceiling: water staining and damage(bedroom and closet/powder-room). Restoration required.
- Walls: seepage through brickwork and staining across the powder-room and bedroom sides of the affected wall. Restoration required.
- **Carpet: flooded and saturated over an extended period. Requires full replacement** — cleaning is not sufficient where the underlay and slab interface have been wet for weeks.
- Wall cabinets replaced at my own cost following the November 2025 event, before the 2026 recurrence.

### Furniture and contents

- **Bed frame, under bed cabinets and items inside — water damaged.**
- **Wall-mounted oak facade/top cabinets** — mould growth on and behind the units. Reported to Unit 61's agent on 11 August 2026: *"the back of the cabinets is fully covered in mould and I was afraid to touch it myself to avoid further contamination."*
- **Contents stored in the affected room and within those cabinets**, across the following categories:
  - **Clothing and footwear** — mould contamination. Textiles and leather exposed to sustained mould are generally not remediable to a wearable condition and require replacement rather than cleaning.
  - **Books and printed material** — mould contamination. Paper does not recover from mould; affected items include material that is out of print or otherwise not replaceable at any price.
  - **Artworks and decorative objects** — mould and damp exposure. Some items are unique.
  - **Electronics** — damp and mould exposure, including damage to internal components.
  - Watches — damp and mould exposure, including damage to mechanism.
  - **Other personal effects and valuables.**
- An **itemised schedule** of the above, with age, replacement cost, purchase evidence and photographs, is held separately and will be provided to the responsible party, the insurer, and the Tribunal as required. It is not reproduced on this page.
- Mould described in correspondence as etched into walls, furniture and carpet rather than surface-level, and not remediable by cleaning.

### Costs and consequential loss

- 2weeks of running heaters and fans continuously to dry the affected area , with associated electricity cost.
- Temporary accommodation required whole June 2026 till early July, and again as at August 2026 when mould contamination became catastrophic
- Time and expense incurred over nine months pursuing investigation, isolation and repair across two email chains, plumbing contractors and multiple follow-ups.
